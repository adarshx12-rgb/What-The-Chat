Who this policy covers

This policy applies to the What The Chat website at whatthechat.com and its browser-based chat studio. For privacy questions or requests, use the details on our contact page.

Chat content and saved projects

The studio processes the messages, contact names, avatars, wallpapers, and images you choose in your browser. Saved projects, including their images and recorded message timing, are stored locally using IndexedDB. The studio does not require an account or provide cloud project storage.

The normal editing and export workflow does not upload your chat project to a What The Chat server. Downloaded screenshots, videos, and JSON backups are files on your device. If you share those files, the recipient or publishing platform will handle them under its own policies.

Import only project backups you trust. Images referenced by an imported project may cause your browser to request the image from its source if the file contains an external image URL.

Browser storage and deletion

The studio uses localStorage for preferences such as the studio theme, notification sound setting, and panel width. Saved projects remain in browser storage until you delete them, clear the site's data, or the browser removes them. Private browsing and browser storage limits can affect persistence.

Delete individual projects from the Projects panel on the homepage. To remove all local projects and preferences, clear this site's data in your browser settings. Export a backup first if you want to keep your work. Clearing browser storage does not delete files you previously downloaded.

The application code does not set advertising cookies or include analytics trackers.

Optional local MP4 conversion

If you run the optional converter, the studio checks its availability at 127.0.0.1:8787 and sends the recorded video to that service on your own computer. The converter writes temporary input and output files and attempts to delete them after conversion and delivery, or after a conversion failure. An interrupted process can leave temporary files behind.

Without the converter, you can download the browser's native recording. The converter does not need an internet upload to perform the conversion.

Website requests and external services

Visiting the hosted website sends ordinary request information, such as your IP address, browser information, requested URL, and request time, to the hosting infrastructure. Hosting providers may keep operational and security logs according to their settings and policies.

The website requests fonts from Google Fonts. The homepage also loads the Tailwind browser styling library from jsDelivr. These providers receive the network information needed to deliver those resources; their own policies govern that processing. See Google's privacy policy and jsDelivr's privacy policy.

Information shared for support

If you contact us, you choose what to send. Your email address, message, and any attachments may be used to answer your request, troubleshoot an issue, and handle follow-up questions. Email providers also process messages to deliver and store them.

Share only what is needed to explain the issue. Do not send passwords, private conversations, or other people's personal information. Support correspondence is kept for as long as needed to handle the request and any necessary follow-up or applicable obligations.

Your choices and requests

You control locally saved projects through your browser and the Projects panel. We cannot retrieve a local project that has not been shared with us. Depending on your location and the information involved, you may also have rights to request access, correction, deletion, or restriction of personal information held about you, and to raise a concern with your local privacy regulator.

Use our contact page for requests about information shared for support. We may need enough information to verify and locate your request.

Changes to this policy

We may update this page when the website or its information practices change. The updated date identifies the latest revision. Review it again if you want to understand how a new feature handles information.
